A logo is small but it carries a lot of responsibility. It appears on signs, screens, packaging, invoices and tiny app icons. A logo that looks great in one place and falls apart in another is not doing its job. Here are seven mistakes to avoid.

## 1. Making it too complicated

Detailed illustrations, thin lines and many colours may look impressive on a large screen, but they turn into a blur when small. Strong logos are simple enough to be recognised at a glance and redrawn from memory.

## 2. Following a trend too closely

Trends fade. A logo built around the style of the moment can look dated within a few years. Aim for something timeless, and let trends show up in how you use the brand, not in the logo itself.

## 3. Using too many fonts or colours

Two fonts at most, and a small, controlled set of colours, keep a logo clean. Each extra element dilutes the idea.

## 4. Ignoring small sizes

Always test your logo at the size of a social media profile picture and on a phone screen. If it cannot be read there, simplify it or create a compact version for small spaces.

## 5. Forgetting black and white

A logo should still work in a single colour, on a light background, a dark background and in black and white. You will need those versions for stamps, embroidery, newspapers and faxed or photocopied documents.

## 6. Copying or borrowing from others

Starting from someone else's logo, or using stock icons with no changes, risks looking generic or even causing legal trouble. A logo is a business asset. It should be original and yours.

## 7. Delivering only one file

A single image is rarely enough. Make sure you receive your logo in the formats and versions you will need. Our guide to [logo file formats](logo-file-formats-explained.html) explains what to ask for.

## How to check your logo

1. Shrink it to the size of a thumbnail. Is it still clear?
2. View it in black on white and white on black.
3. Cover the name. Does the symbol still feel distinctive?
4. Ask someone to look at it for five seconds, then describe it. What did they remember?
