Almost every design project involves changes. A first draft is a conversation starter, not a final answer. The question is how to handle revisions so the work improves instead of going in circles.

## Why revision rounds exist

Designers set a number of revision rounds so that a project has a clear finish line. Without one, feedback can stretch forever, and the work keeps shifting. A defined number protects the client's budget and the designer's time.

Many designers include a small number of rounds in the price, for example two. Anything beyond that is often charged separately. Ask about this at the start, and make sure it is written down.

## What counts as a revision

It helps to separate two things.

- **A revision** is a change to the existing direction, such as a different colour, moved text or a corrected detail.
- **A new direction** is starting over, such as a new concept or a completely different style.

A new direction is a new piece of work, so it usually has a separate cost. This is why a clear brief at the beginning matters so much. Our post on [how to brief a designer](how-to-brief-a-designer.html) covers it.

## How to make each round count

Revisions go faster when feedback is well prepared.

1. **Collect all comments in one go.** Sending changes one by one, over several days, uses up rounds quickly.
2. **Gather feedback from everyone first.** If three people have opinions, combine them before sending, so the designer is not pulled in different directions.
3. **Be specific.** "Make the title bigger and move the phone number to the bottom" helps more than "it needs more life."
4. **Explain the reason.** A reason lets the designer solve the problem in the best way, not just follow an instruction.

## Know what to approve early

Large decisions, such as the concept, colours and layout, should be settled in the early rounds. Small details, such as spelling and spacing, come later. Changing the whole idea at the final stage is the most common reason projects overrun.

## Check the details before final approval

Before saying yes, read every word, check names, numbers, dates and phone numbers, and view the design on the device where it will be seen. Once files are approved and delivered, further changes may count as new work.

## Be fair both ways

A good working relationship is built on clarity. Designers should explain the process and respond promptly. Clients should give timely, organised feedback.
