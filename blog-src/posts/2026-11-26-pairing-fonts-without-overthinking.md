Font choice can feel like a maze. There are thousands of typefaces, and every list online seems to disagree. The truth is that most good designs use very few fonts, and the pairing rules are simpler than they look.

## Start with two, not five

For almost any project, two typefaces are enough: one for headlines and one for body text. Sometimes a single family used in different weights is all you need. Every extra font adds noise and makes the design feel less certain.

## Create contrast, not confusion

Two fonts work when they are clearly different, and fail when they are *almost* the same. If both fonts are similar sans-serifs, the pairing looks like a mistake. Instead, make the difference obvious:

- A bold, expressive headline font with a quiet, readable body font.
- A serif with a sans-serif.
- A wide font with a narrow one.

Pick one font to be the star and let the other support it.

## Match the mood to the message

Fonts carry personality before anyone reads the words. A rounded, friendly typeface suits a children's bakery. A sharp, high-contrast serif feels more like fashion or luxury. A clean geometric sans feels modern and technical. Ask what the brand should feel like, then choose type that agrees with it.

## Keep body text boring

Body text exists to be read, not admired. Choose something simple and highly legible, and set it at a comfortable size with enough space between the lines. Save the decorative fonts for short headlines, where character matters and reading effort does not.

## Use weight and size to build hierarchy

You can create a whole system from one family. Use bold for headings, regular for body, and light or italic for small notes. Combined with a few sizes, this gives structure without adding another typeface.

## Check that the font suits where it is used

- **Small sizes.** Thin, fancy fonts can disappear on a phone screen.
- **Print.** Make sure the font is licensed for commercial use.
- **Languages.** If you write names or text with special characters, confirm the font includes them.

## Mind the licence

Not every font is free for business use. Fonts from trusted libraries often include clear licences, but always check before using one on a logo, a product or a paid project.

## A simple recipe to try

1. Choose a headline font with strong personality.
2. Choose a neutral, readable sans-serif or serif for body text.
3. Set up three sizes: large, medium and small.
4. Use only those, everywhere, and see how calm the result feels.
