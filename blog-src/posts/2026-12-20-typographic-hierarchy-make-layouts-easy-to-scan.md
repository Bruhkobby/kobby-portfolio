Look at any page that feels easy to read and you will find hierarchy. It is the order of importance built into a layout, and it tells the reader where to start, where to go next and what can wait.

## Why hierarchy matters

People rarely read a design line by line. They scan. If everything is the same size and weight, there is no entry point, and the eye wanders. With good hierarchy, the reader understands the structure in a second and then chooses what to read.

## The main tools

You can build hierarchy using a handful of simple controls.

### Size

Larger things look more important. A headline should be clearly bigger than a subheading, and a subheading bigger than body text. Make the steps noticeable. Tiny differences look like mistakes.

### Weight

Bold text stands out against regular text. Use it for headings, key words and labels, and keep it rare so it keeps its effect.

### Colour and contrast

A strong colour or a high-contrast element draws the eye. Use your accent colour for the things that matter most, such as a headline or a button.

### Space

Space groups and separates. Items that are close together feel related. Extra space around an element gives it importance. Giving a headline room makes it stronger than making it bigger.

### Position

In many languages, people read from the top left towards the bottom right. Placing key items at the top, or at the start of a line, takes advantage of that habit.

## Build three levels

A simple approach is to design three levels.

1. **Primary.** The headline or main message. There should be only one.
2. **Secondary.** Subheadings, dates, prices and key details.
3. **Tertiary.** Body text, notes and small print.

If you can describe your layout as these three levels, and each is clearly different from the others, the hierarchy is working.

## Avoid common problems

- **Too many emphasis styles.** Bold, italics, colour, underline and capitals together make the page noisy.
- **Competing focal points.** Two huge elements fight each other. Choose one winner.
- **Low contrast.** Pale text on a pale background loses its place in the order. Our post on [colour contrast](colour-contrast-for-designers.html) explains how to check it.
- **Cramped spacing.** Without room, even a good hierarchy turns into clutter.

## A simple test

Squint at your design, or look at it from across the room. The things that stand out should be the things you want people to notice first. If something unimportant dominates, adjust it.
