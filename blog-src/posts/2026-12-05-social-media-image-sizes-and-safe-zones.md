Few things are more frustrating than posting a design and seeing the text cut off at the edges. Platforms crop, resize and cover parts of your image with buttons and captions. A little planning at the start prevents all of it.

## Why sizes matter

Every platform shows images in particular shapes. If your design does not match, the platform will crop it or add empty bars. The shape you design in is called the aspect ratio, and it matters more than the exact pixel count.

## Common formats

Platforms change their layouts from time to time, so confirm the current recommendation before a big campaign. These shapes are widely used:

- **Square (1:1).** Works in most feeds and is a safe all-round choice.
- **Portrait (4:5).** Takes more room on a phone screen, which helps your post stand out in a feed.
- **Vertical (9:16).** Used for stories, reels and short videos.
- **Landscape (16:9).** Common for video and for link previews.

If you are unsure, start by designing at a high resolution so you can crop down without losing sharpness.

## What is a safe zone?

A safe zone is the area of your design that is guaranteed to stay visible. Everything important, such as text, logos and faces, should live inside it. The outer edges are for background and decoration only.

Vertical formats need extra care. The top of a story or reel is often covered by the profile name and progress bar, and the bottom by captions, buttons and reply fields. Leave generous space at both ends.

## Simple rules to follow

1. **Keep a margin.** Leave space around the edges, roughly a tenth of the width, for text and logos.
2. **Centre your key message.** The middle of the frame is the safest place.
3. **Think about the grid.** Many profiles show a cropped centre square of each post, so make sure the main idea survives that crop.
4. **Test on a phone.** Preview before posting, and look at the post in the feed and on your profile.

## Keep text readable

Most people view posts on small screens, quickly. Use large type, strong contrast and short lines. If you have to zoom in to read it, it is too small.

## Export well

Export at a good resolution and in a suitable format. Photos usually work well as JPG and graphics with flat colours or sharp text often look better as PNG. Avoid heavily compressed files, since platforms compress them again.
