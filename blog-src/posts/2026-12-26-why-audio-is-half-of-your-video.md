Most people think about video as something you see. Yet when sound is poor, viewers leave faster than they do for poor picture. A slightly soft image can feel charming. A voice that is muffled, noisy or uneven feels like hard work.

## Clear voice comes first

If someone is speaking, their voice is the most important sound in the video. A few habits make a big difference:

- **Record close to the mouth.** Distance adds echo and background noise.
- **Pick a quiet place.** Fans, traffic and generators are easier to avoid than to remove.
- **Use a dedicated microphone** when possible. A simple lapel or small external mic usually beats a phone's built-in one.
- **Do a test.** Record ten seconds and listen with headphones before filming the whole thing.

## Level the volume

Nothing annoys viewers more than having to adjust the volume. In editing, make speech consistent from start to finish, so quiet and loud moments are balanced. Aim for natural-sounding speech that is comfortably easy to hear on a phone speaker, because that is where many videos are played.

## Clean up noise

Light background hiss or hum can often be reduced in editing. Heavy noise is much harder to fix without damaging the voice, so it is better to prevent it while recording. Use noise reduction gently, because too much makes speech sound strange and robotic.

## Choose music that supports, not competes

Music sets the mood. Fast, bright music creates excitement. Soft, slow music feels warm or emotional. Choose a track that fits the message and keep it behind the voice so words remain clear. When someone is talking, lower the music, and bring it up again in the pauses.

Always use music you have the right to use. Many free-looking tracks have conditions, and a copyright claim can get a video muted or removed.

## Add small sound effects

Light touches such as a soft whoosh on a transition or a click on a button can make an edit feel polished. Use them sparingly. If viewers notice every effect, there are too many.

## Silence has value

Gaps in sound can be powerful. A brief pause before an important line, or a moment of natural room sound, gives the audience time to take something in. Not every second needs to be filled.

## Listen on more than one device

Check your video on headphones, a phone speaker and, if possible, a laptop. A video that sounds fine in studio headphones can be thin or boomy elsewhere. Our guide to [pacing and rhythm](pacing-and-rhythm-in-video-editing.html) pairs well with good sound, since the two work together.
