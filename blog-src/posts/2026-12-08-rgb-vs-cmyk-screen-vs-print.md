You finish a bright design, send it to the printer, and the colours come back looking a little flatter than you expected. This is one of the most common surprises in design, and it has a simple explanation.

## Two ways to make colour

**RGB** stands for red, green and blue. Screens make colour by shining light, and mixing those three lights creates every colour you see on a phone, laptop or TV. More light means a brighter result.

**CMYK** stands for cyan, magenta, yellow and key (black). Printers make colour by putting ink on paper. Paper reflects light, and ink absorbs some of it. More ink means a darker result.

Because they work so differently, each has its own range of colours it can produce, called a gamut. RGB can show some very bright, vivid colours, especially strong blues, greens and glowing oranges, that CMYK ink cannot match.

## What this means for you

When an RGB design is converted to CMYK, the brightest colours are pulled into the range that ink can reproduce. The change is often small, but on vivid colours it can be noticeable.

## Which should you use?

- **Digital work** such as websites, social posts and video should be made in RGB.
- **Print work** such as flyers, banners, business cards and packaging should be prepared for CMYK.

If a design will appear in both places, it is smart to start in RGB for flexibility, then create a print version and check the colours carefully.

## Practical tips

1. **Choose the mode at the start.** Setting the document to CMYK for print means you see a closer preview from the beginning.
2. **Avoid relying on neon colours for print.** Ink usually cannot match them. Pick a slightly deeper shade you know will print well.
3. **Check black.** For large areas of rich black, ask the printer for their preferred recipe. For small text, use plain black so edges stay sharp.
4. **Print a proof.** Before a big order, print one copy or a small run and check it in daylight.
5. **Remember the paper.** Glossy and matte paper change how colour looks. Uncoated paper usually looks softer.

## Screens differ too

Even on screens, colour changes. Phones have different brightness and colour settings, so what you see is not exactly what everyone sees. That is another reason to test.
